# Meetings

A provider-neutral meeting entry point on the hill behind Work. The painted radar dish and participant monitor express communication rather than any vendor logo.

- **Peek:** independent station sprite; no invented meeting count or active-call animation.
- **Open:** one paper sheet (`open.ts`). **Upcoming** lists the next calls from Google Calendar that carry a Google Meet, Zoom or Teams link (time, title, service and who is invited; a call already running reads *Now* and *Join now*). **New meeting** starts a call on Google Meet (`meet.google.com/new`, an instant meeting link), Zoom (web client home) or Microsoft Teams, on the service's own page: nothing is created through an account grant, so the Calendar connection stays read-only (no new Google scope). A meeting link pasted into **Join with a link** opens only when it is a recognized call link. Website sign-in and account switching belong to those services. Cookies use the existing shared browser store; isolated profiles and multiple Google OAuth grants are not implemented.
- **Focus:** the chosen call's web client in Worldlet's website panel (the CEF engine where it is built), full-screen radar landscape behind it, Fox at right. Back restores Open. No automatic Join click or native app launch. Meet, Zoom and Teams pages may ask for the camera and microphone (owner report 2026-10-05: a call had neither). Worldlet asks the person nothing itself (owner request 2026-10-07: the OS asks); it asks macOS (or reads Windows' privacy switch) for Worldlet itself, and says where to turn a blocked device on; the page keeps what was allowed while it stays on that site, and the site then reads the devices as allowed, so the call stops asking on its own (`platform/electron/src/modules/browser/device.ts`, `media-access.ts`). Other sites get neither. Real call compatibility and permissions require service/device acceptance.
- **Brief:** opening a calendar call (from Upcoming, or Join on the due-call offer) asks Fox once per call within half an hour for a short brief: what the call is about, who is coming and what the World knows about them (emails, notes, calendar, earlier meeting transcripts), and what to prepare. Fox's context while the call is open names its title, time, organizer, invited people, location and invite text (marked untrusted, plain text, bounded); **Brief** beside Fox asks again. New calls and pasted links open without a brief. The sample world never asks.

## Live transcript

Owner request 2026-10-04: an exception to "no screen or microphone recording" for meetings only. Since 2026-10-06 it starts with the call (owner request: 「把 Meetings 转写改成进会自动开始，Stop 后本场不再自动开」).

- **Start and stop.** It starts by itself once a call page in Meetings can be heard (the host reports `meeting-ready` when the page's hooks are in and it shows a Meet, Zoom or Teams address); **Transcribe** beside Fox starts it again. After the person stops it, or when it could not start, it stays off until they open another call. The practice world never transcribes, and Fox can never start it (`browserCommand transcribe` refuses Fox). It ends with **Stop**, with **Stop transcript** beside Fox, or when the call's page leaves the panel (Back, another Applet, the window closing).
- **Always visible.** While it runs, a red **Transcribing** bar sits above the call, with Stop, the newest line, and a reminder to let everyone in the call know.
- **What is heard.** On the CEF engine, a Meetings page starts blank, and its call-audio hooks (`platform/bridge/meeting-audio.js`) are installed at document start before the call loads (`engine/page.ts`). They take the person's microphone from the site's own `getUserMedia`. They take everyone else from the WebRTC tracks the page receives and from what the page plays through Web Audio (Zoom's web client). Nothing is captured before Start. The hooks send 16 kHz mono PCM of each side to the host about once a second, through a binding with a random name. Other sites get no hooks. Electron's fallback views do not transcribe.
- **Text only.** Each side is cut into windows: at the first quiet second after 6 s, or at 25 s. Silence is dropped, and the windows are transcribed one at a time by local Whisper Large v3 Turbo, auto-detecting the language (`core/applets/meeting-transcript.ts`, `browser/meeting-transcript.ts`). Audio is released once its text is known. When far behind, the oldest audio is skipped and the bar says so.
- **Kept in the World.** Each line is a `transcript` record (`You: …` or `Others: …`, with the meeting's title and a session id) in the call's website visit in `world.sqlite`. It is searchable, and it is deleted with that site's recordings in Settings › Privacy and data. Transcript lines are not raw network data, so they are not pruned after 30 days.
- **Reading it.** The sheet lists **Transcripts**, and each opens its text. Fox finds them with `browser/records` and reads them with `browser/record` and `only:"transcript"`, for notes, action items, or the brief before the next call with the same people.
- **A summary after every call.** Owner request 2026-10-06. When a transcript ends (Stop, or the call's page leaving the panel) and its last line is kept, the World asks Fox once for that transcript's summary (`meetingSummaryRequest` in `core/applets/meetings.ts`, shown as *Meeting summary*). Fox reads the whole transcript from the call's visit and shows a large [artifact](../../../core/artifacts/README.md) with Summary, Decisions, Action items and Open questions, plus up to three next steps such as a follow-up email. Like every artifact it is kept in `world.sqlite` and listed in the Journal page of Fox's panel. Fox uses the person's own model path (local Codex sign-in, their API key, or a local Agent); with none, Fox offers *Choose a model* as for any request. Reading the transcript needs private-context consent, like any `browser/records` read. Transcripts with no lines, the practice world, and calls whose app closed before the transcript ended get no summary.

The check is `node scripts/meeting-transcript-check.ts` (windows, storage and read-back, and the hooks hearing both sides of a real WebRTC call in Chromium). A real Meet, Zoom or Teams call in the app needs device acceptance.

## Timed entry

The existing Google primary-calendar source exposes start/end, conference entry points, description and attendance state. Refresh at most once per minute while the app runs; check due calls every five seconds. Auto-entry is disabled for Sample, hidden windows and onboarding. Only recognized HTTPS Meet/Zoom/Teams call URLs are eligible, with no credentials or nonstandard ports. Exclude all-day, cancelled and self-declined events. Open only within two minutes of start, with a successful source refresh less than 90 seconds old. Do not replace an already open meeting. Remember handled occurrence IDs (event ID + start) locally, bounded to 200; no tokens or meeting URLs are persisted by this scheduler. No OS wake scheduling or closed-app/background-window alerts.

The local unit check is `node scripts/meetings-check.ts`. Actual third-party calls are not implied by fixture coverage.
