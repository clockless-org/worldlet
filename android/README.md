# Worldlet for Android

The Android companion (owner request 2026-10-03): the same one screen as the [iPhone app](../ios/README.md), built the Android way in Kotlin and Jetpack Compose. The **Attention Center** sits over the dimmed world in three layers stacked top to bottom (owner decision 2026-10-03): the **Applet world**, **Now** in its three groups (Coming Up, Worth Doing, Worth Knowing, with the computer's blue, yellow and green and its diamond, rounded-square and circle marks), where it opens, and **Later**, under one frosted Applets/Now/Later capsule (no counts, owner decision 2026-10-04). Swipe down on Now (at the top of its content) to rise to the Applet world and up to turn to Later; from Later swipe down at the top of its list, and from the Applet world swipe up, to come back to Now. Each layer scrolls on its own and hands the gesture on only at its end, and a tap on a tab goes straight there. The Applet world is the World's Applets as the computer sends them, in a four-column grid of frosted tiles under Working now, Accounts, Ongoing (conversations brought from other Agents that the person made into Applets) and In your World: a lantern ring breathes around an Applet at work (still when animations are off), a red dot marks one that failed, one that is off is faded, and a yellow badge counts the Now items it holds. A widget's tile opens the widget; any other tile opens the **Applet page**, which rises from the bottom over the Center and stops above Fox: the Applet with what it is doing, **Open on computer**, the items it brought to the Center (each opens its card) and its latest world log lines. A drag down on its handle, Back or ✕ returns to where it was opened from. A card whose item has a home Applet shows "From <Applet> ›" by its source, which opens that Applet's page in the card's place. The round computer button at the top right, with a green dot while the computer is connected, opens Settings in a bottom sheet, as Fox does. Tapping a row slides its card down from the top on paper: the item's illustration (the same pictures the computer's card uses), its kind, title, when and source, the saved brief, and Later, Dismiss and a filled Done or Got it. Each choice sends the card flying the way it points (Later up, Dismiss left, Done right) and brings down the next item's card; the close button, a swipe up or a tap beside the card closes it. A long press on a row offers Done, Later and Remove. **Fox** sits at the bottom as one input bar under its paper dialogue box (owner request 2026-10-04, like Claude's): Fox's portrait is a round button at the bar's left, a little taller than the bar, that opens Settings, then the line to type, the microphone and Send; with a card open the dialogue is that item's conversation, with the option Fox offers. With an Applet page open, a paper pill above the bar says "In <Applet>" (its ✕ closes the page), lines typed or spoken go to that Applet's own thread of the conversation, and the dialogue shows that thread; a card opened over the page takes over until it closes. While Fox works on the computer, its current thinking step and then its reply stream into the box word by word (the relay's `live` slot), as on the computer and the iPhone. At rest the bar is a little shorter and says "Tap to type · hold to speak": a tap opens the keyboard, and holding the bar talks until released, as holding the microphone does. The microphone takes a tap (tap it again, or Send, to send) or a hold (release to send); Send or the keyboard's send key sends a typed line. Speech is recognized on the phone only (Android's on-device recognizer, Android 12 and later) and sent as text; a phone without on-device recognition says voice is off rather than sending audio anywhere. A long press on Fox's button starts **Talk with Fox** (`TalkMode`, [Talk with Fox](../ui/companion/CONVERSATION.md#talk-with-fox)): Fox listens, sends each thing said once the recognizer's words have stopped changing for 1.2 seconds (the kit's `TalkPause`), reads its finished reply aloud with TextToSpeech in the reply's language, and listens again; while Talk is on, Fox's ring and the microphone wear lantern gold, the line says whether Fox listens, thinks or speaks, while Fox speaks the microphone stays open through communication mode, with the voice played as voice communication so the phone’s echo cancellation covers it and words of the person's own, not Fox's echo (`TalkRules.interruption`), stop the voice and become the next line, a tap on the microphone interrupts it too, a short "be quiet" (安静点) keeps Talk without the voice (`TalkRules`), and ✕ in the bar or another long press ends it. No wake word yet. While the computer takes [Orders](../core/phone/README.md#payloads), a round bug button (Order) stands on its own left of the bar (owner request 2026-10-06): a tap listens and turns it red, a second tap sends the words to the computer as an Order for the team's Claude, never to Fox; when nothing was heard a short line above it says so.

**Moment Applets** (Applets Fox makes for one moment, [core/widgets](../core/widgets/README.md)) lead Now and stand in the Applet world: each one is a row in its accent colour with its name, one line about it and "Until 6:00 PM" or "Pinned". Tapping one opens its page full screen in a sandboxed WebView (JavaScript on, network, file and content access off, WebRTC removed from its window before the page runs, navigation and new windows refused, loaded from a string with no base URL); the page's local storage comes from `WorldletAndroid.seed()` and every change it reports through `WorldletAndroid.post` is stamped with the phone's clock, kept and sent to the computer as a `widget` message. The computer's `widgets` slot merges in newest-wins per key, and an open page whose values changed reloads where it was scrolled. The last slot, with each page and the phone's own state, is kept in `widgets.json` in the app's files (left out of backups and device transfers), so made Applets open after a relaunch while the computer sleeps; one whose page did not fit in the slot says to open it on the computer.

It pairs with Worldlet on the computer by QR code (the companion panel's **Mobile** page): Google's code scanner reads it without Worldlet holding the camera permission, the camera app can open the `worldlet://pair` link directly, or the link can be pasted. It talks to the computer through the worldlet.ai relay with end-to-end encryption, exactly as the iPhone does; the computer does all the work and the phone never signs in to anything. The pairing is sealed with a key kept in the Android Keystore and left out of backups, so a new phone pairs again. Protocol, relay and payloads: [Phone pairing](../core/phone/README.md).

**Approvals** (contracts/harness-services.ts `approvals`): when the person's own Agent asks before it acts (a Hermes Agent command, an OpenClaw exec request), the live turn carries the request ([phone pairing](../core/phone/README.md#payloads)), and Fox's dialogue shows it on the turn's card as the computer does: the request, its command in a small code box, and Allow once, Always and Deny in the style of Fox's option. A request from an item's thread is also its own card in the main conversation, where the notification opens. Its notification carries Allow once and Deny itself ([Notifications](#notifications)). A tap sends the `approval` message and the card keeps the settled line ("Allowed once.", "Denied."); a failed send brings the buttons back. A turn that waits on an approval stays shown for ten minutes (kit `HarnessApproval`, `LiveTurn.asking`, `LiveTurn.shown`; tests in `PairingTest`).

## Notifications

While the app is closed, the computer can tell the phone when a Now item arrives, a routine or background task reports, Fox answers a line sent from the phone, or the person's own Agent asks permission (its tap opens Fox's dialogue with the approval card) ([phone pairing](../core/phone/README.md)), the messages people who used OpenClaw or Hermes Agent got on Telegram (owner goal 2026-10-07). The computer seals the notification for this pairing (place `push`) and the relay hands Firebase Cloud Messaging only a data message `{p, b}`: the pairing id and the sealed box. `PushService` opens the box with the pairing key from `PairingStore` and posts the notification itself on the `worldlet` channel, so neither the relay nor Google sees what it says; a box for another pairing, or one that does not open, shows nothing, and nothing is posted while the app is on screen, where the Center and Fox already show it. A tap opens the app on its target: the item's card, the Applet's page or Fox's dialogue (`PushTarget`, carried in the launch intent's extras).

The notification's own buttons come from the sealed `act` (kit `PhonePush.actions`, [Actions](../core/phone/README.md#actions)): Done and Later on an item, Reply with inline text (`RemoteInput`) on Fox's answer, Allow once and Deny on what the person's own Agent asks (Deny alone when Allow once is not offered). Each is an explicit broadcast to the app's own `PushActionReceiver` (not exported) carrying the opened push and the pairing id; it sends the same message as the app's own button (`PhonePush.message`) through the relay without opening the app, then removes the notification, or posts it again with its buttons and "Not sent" when the message could not go. A push from an earlier pairing does nothing. Reply and Allow once ask to unlock the phone first (`setAuthenticationRequired`); Reply's intent is the only mutable one, as `RemoteInput` needs.

After pairing, Android 13 and later ask once, with one line on what notifications are for. Each time the pairing connects (and after that answer) the app gives the relay its Firebase token with `PUT /api/pair/<id>/push` (platform `fcm`), or takes it back with `DELETE` while notifications are off; a new token goes the same way from `onNewToken`, and unpairing deletes the token on the relay before the pairing. Firebase is set up from the build, not from a `google-services.json` and without the Google services plugin: `FCM_PROJECT_ID`, `FCM_APP_ID`, `FCM_API_KEY` and `FCM_SENDER_ID` (the Firebase project `worldlet`'s Android app; not secrets, every build carries them) are written in `app/build.gradle.kts`, and Gradle properties (`-P` or `~/.gradle/gradle.properties`) or the environment replace them in `BuildConfig`. A build with an empty one has no push and behaves as before: Firebase's start-up provider is removed and its auto-init is off, so nothing starts and no token exists until a paired phone with notifications allowed asks for one. The relay needs the matching `FCM_SERVICE_ACCOUNT` to send.

## Build and test

- `./gradlew :kit:test :app:testDebugUnitTest` here runs the protocol tests and the demo UI tests on any computer with a JDK 17 or later and the Android SDK (`ANDROID_HOME` or `local.properties`).
- `./gradlew :app:assembleDebug` builds `app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk`; `:app:assembleRelease` builds a shrunk release APK.
- **Try the demo** on the pairing screen ("No computer yet? Try the demo", as on the iPhone, so Play review and people without Worldlet on a computer can see the app) loads the sample data with no computer and no network, in every build: Done, Later and Dismiss work on it, Fox answers every line with a note that this is the demo, and Settings offers Leave the demo, which goes back to pairing. It is not kept, so the app opens on pairing next time.
- Demo mode from launch extras (debug builds only, for tests and screenshots): `adb shell am start -n app.worldlet.android/.MainActivity --ez demo true`, with `--es page applets` (or `now`, `later`) for that layer of the Center, `--es applet mail` for an Applet's page, `--es card t1` (or `e1`, with an earlier turn) for an item's card, or `--ez live true` for a reply Fox is still streaming.
- `npm run test:android` (repository root) is the Windows RC's Android gate: it sets up a JDK and the Android SDK, emulator and system image when they are missing (outside the checkout, in `~/.worldlet-android` unless an SDK already exists), runs the JVM tests, boots an emulator without a window, runs the instrumented UI tests and keeps the APKs and pictures of each screen in `.local/android-build`. It is advisory until it has passed on 01. So 01 tests Windows and Android and 02 tests Mac and iPhone.
- Pull requests run nothing for the Android app (owner decision 2026-10-04: PR CI is Linux basic checks only). The protocol tests, the builds, the JVM UI tests and the instrumented UI tests run in the Windows RC as `npm run test:android`.

## Distribution

On Google Play as "Worldlet AI" (`app.worldlet.android`, Play Console account of Clockless LLC, an organization account, so no closed-test period was required). 2026.1005.2799 was sent for production review on 2026-10-05 (owner request) and is live on Google Play since 2026-10-05; the review notes point to Try the demo. Play App Signing holds the app signing key; the upload key is on the development host (03) in `~/.worldlet-android/upload` (`worldlet-upload.jks` and its `keystore.properties`; copy that file to `android/keystore.properties` to sign), and the store text, icon, feature graphic and screenshots are in `~/.worldlet-android/play`. A release is `./gradlew :app:bundleRelease` on a main commit, uploaded to the Production track with release notes. `versionCode` is the commit's position on main and `versionName` the Pacific day plus that number (`2026.1004.2716`), the same Build as the desktop and iPhone builds of that commit, so nobody edits them.

Every pull request that touches the app also keeps a debug APK and a release APK as the `android-apk` workflow artifact, and every Windows RC keeps them in `.local/android-build`; either installs on a phone with installation from unknown sources allowed. Without `android/keystore.properties` (`storeFile`, `storePassword`, `keyAlias`, `keyPassword`; kept out of Git) the release build is signed with the debug key.
