# Worldlet for iPhone

A native SwiftUI app with one screen that looks like the computer's world (owner request 2026-10-03): the **Attention Center** over the dimmed world, and **Fox** resident at the bottom. The Center is the computer's, in its own style, in three layers one above the other (owner decision 2026-10-03): the **Applet world** on top, **Now** in the middle, where the app opens, in its three groups (Coming Up, Worth Doing, Worth Knowing, with the same blue, yellow and green and the same diamond, rounded-square and circle marks), and **Later** below. Now is one screen: a long Now first drops its rows' second line, then the reasons, and when even the titles do not fit (a small iPhone on a busy day) they scroll inside Now, never showing through the next layer. Swipe down on Now for the Applet world and up for Later (when Now scrolls, pull past its top or its end); pull Later down past its top, or the Applet world up past its end, to come back to Now. The system's segmented control at the top holds the three (a grid mark, then Now and Later, with no counts: owner decision 2026-10-04); on iOS 26 its selection is the system's Liquid Glass. Only the layers' offset follows the finger, so a swipe does not rebuild the pages; the world backdrop is blurred ahead of time and nothing over it casts a live shadow, so nothing is redrawn offscreen while a layer moves (owner report 2026-10-04: swiping stuttered). Later is grouped like Now, with rows a little smaller and closer together but just as readable, under a quiet note; its list scrolls freely. The **Applet world** is the World behind the Center brought forward as a grid of its Applets, as the computer sends them: Working now (widgets for now, Applets at work and those holding Now items), then Accounts, then Ongoing (conversations brought from other Agents that the person made into Applets, core/ongoing), then In your World. Each tile is the Applet's own device from the World, the computer's Peek art (owner request 2026-10-04; `Assets.xcassets/Applets`, made by `scripts/ios-applet-icons.mjs` from the built-in style's manifest; Moment Applets and Ongoing things show their own devices). A tile breathes a lantern glow while its Applet works, like the device's lamp on the computer, shows a red dot when it failed and fades when it is off, and its badge counts its Now items. A widget opens its page; any other tile opens the Applet's **page**, which rises over the Center and stops above Fox: what the Applet is doing, On computer (opens it there), the items it brought to the Center and its latest lines from the world log. Every card names the Applet the item came from ("From Mail ›"), which opens that page in the card's place; a swipe down or the close button goes back to where it was opened from. The round computer button at the top right, with a green dot while the computer is connected, opens Settings (so does Fox). Tapping a row slides its card down from the top, drawn the iOS way in the computer's colours: the item's illustration across its head (the same pictures the computer's card uses), its kind, title, when and source, the saved brief, and Later, Dismiss and a prominent Done or Got it at its foot. Each choice sends the card flying the way it points (Later up, Dismiss left, Done right; a dismissed item stays) and brings down the next item's card; the close button, a swipe up or a tap anywhere outside the card (including beside Fox's input bar) closes it. Holding a row offers Done, Later and Remove. Fox sits at the bottom in one input bar (owner request 2026-10-04): Fox's portrait is a round button at the bar's left, a little taller than the bar, and opens Settings (its ring breathes in lantern light while Fox works and turns red while the microphone listens); then come the line to type, the microphone and Send. Above the bar is Fox's dialogue box, the same paper box as on the computer (not a chat thread), its tail pointing at Fox: the front card shows what the person said and Fox's answer, and History unfolds the earlier ones. While Fox works, its thinking (the step it is on) and then its reply stream in word by word, as on the computer (owner request 2026-10-03). At rest the bar is a little shorter and says "Tap to type · hold to speak": a tap opens the keyboard (Send or Return sends the line), and talking works like a Telegram voice message (owner request 2026-10-03): hold the microphone (or the bar) to talk and release to send, slide sideways to cancel, or slide up to lock and then tap Send to send or ✕ to cancel; a quick tap on the microphone starts a locked recording. While it records, a red dot with the time and a sound wave that rises with the voice take the line's place in the bar. Inside an Applet's page, a small pill above the bar says where it is ("In Mail", with ✕ to leave), and the dialogue is that Applet's own thread: what is said there opens the Applet on the computer and joins its thread. The dialogue follows the open card: with a card open it is that item's conversation, starting with what Fox says about it and the option it offers, and what the person says there joins that item's conversation (the computer opens the same card). The words are recognized on the iPhone only and sent as text, with no audio recorded, uploaded or kept. Holding Fox's button opens its menu with **Talk with Fox** (`TalkMode`, [Talk with Fox](../ui/companion/CONVERSATION.md#talk-with-fox)): Fox listens, sends each thing said once the recognizer's words have stopped changing for 1.2 seconds (`TalkPause`), reads its finished reply aloud with AVSpeechSynthesizer in the reply's language, and listens again; while Talk is on, Fox's ring and the microphone wear lantern gold, the line says whether Fox listens, thinks or speaks, while Fox speaks the microphone stays open through the iPhone’s voice processing (the `.voiceChat` audio mode a call uses) and words of the person's own, not Fox's echo (`TalkRules.interruption`), stop the voice and become the next line, a tap on the microphone interrupts it too, a short "be quiet" (安静点) keeps Talk without the voice (`TalkRules`), and ✕ in the bar (or the menu again, or leaving the dock) ends it. No wake word yet. While the computer takes [Orders](../core/phone/README.md#payloads) (an Alpha or Dev app that can send them), a round bug button (Order) stands on its own left of the bar (owner request 2026-10-06): a tap listens and turns it red, a second tap sends the words to the computer as an Order for the team's Claude, never to Fox; when nothing was heard a short line above it says so. Controls are the system's own where one fits (owner request 2026-10-04): the segmented control, bordered and prominent buttons, navigation bars and Forms; round buttons over the world use Liquid Glass on iOS 26 and later and the bordered style before that. It pairs with Worldlet on the computer by QR code and talks to it through the worldlet.ai relay with end-to-end encryption; the computer does all the work, and the phone never signs in to anything. Changes reach the phone as they happen while the app is open; when the computer is away, the phone keeps showing what it sent last and says when that was. Changes reach the phone as they happen while the app is open, and [notifications](#notifications) while it is closed; when the computer is away, the phone keeps showing what it sent last and says when that was. Protocol, relay and payloads: [Phone pairing](../core/phone/README.md).

**Moment Applets** ([core/widgets](../core/widgets/README.md)): the Applets Fox makes for one moment (a tour guide, a packing list) lead Now and stand in the Applet world, each as a row with its accent, title, blurb and end ("Until 6:00 PM", or Pinned). Tapping one opens it full screen with the system navigation bar (its name and Done) over a `WKWebView` that is sandboxed: a non-persistent data store, a content rule list blocking every http(s) and ws(s) load (if it cannot be compiled the page is not shown), WebRTC removed from its window before the page runs, the page loaded from a string with no base URL, navigation and new windows refused, no link previews. Its storage is Worldlet's: a `WKUserScript` sets `window.__worldletWidgetSeed` (stored values and scroll) before the page runs, and the page reports its values through the `worldletWidget` message handler. The phone stamps changed keys with its clock, keeps them and sends a `widget` message with only those entries; the computer's `widgets` slot merges in newest-wins (`mergeWidgetState` in WorldletKit), so edits made here stay, and the open page reloads where it was scrolled when the computer's values change it. The last slot, with this phone's edits and the pages it has (a page the slot leaves out is kept for the same version), is kept in `widgets.json` in Application Support (left out of backups), so a made Applet works at the museum while the computer sleeps; it is put away at its end, or when the slot no longer lists it. One whose page the phone does not have says to open it on the computer.

**Approvals** (contracts/harness-services.ts `approvals`): when the person's own Agent asks before it acts (a Hermes Agent command, an OpenClaw exec request), the live turn carries the request ([phone pairing](../core/phone/README.md#payloads)), and Fox's dialogue shows it on the turn's card as the computer does: the request, its command in a small code box, and Allow once, Always and Deny in the style of Fox's option. A request from an item's thread is also its own card in the main conversation, where the notification opens. Its notification carries Allow once and Deny itself ([Notifications](#notifications)). A tap sends the `approval` message and the card keeps the settled line ("Allowed once.", "Denied."); a failed send brings the buttons back. A turn that waits on an approval stays shown for ten minutes (WorldletKit `HarnessApproval`, `LiveTurn.asking`, `LiveTurn.shown`; tests in `PublicAPITests`).

## Notifications

What used to reach people on Telegram from OpenClaw or Hermes Agent (owner goal 2026-10-07: cron results, replies) reaches this iPhone as notifications while Worldlet is closed: new Coming Up and Worth Doing items, Fox's answer to a line sent from the phone, what the person's own Agent asks permission for, routines and background tasks. The computer decides what is worth one and seals it; the relay passes it to APNs and never sees the words ([Phone pairing](../core/phone/README.md) owns the protocol, `/push` and `/notify`).

- **Asking.** Once, the first time this iPhone connects to a computer, an alert says in one line what notifications are for (Turn On or Not now); Turn On brings the system's own question. Every later connect, and every new pairing, registers with APNs when they are allowed. The demo never asks.
- **Token.** `AppDelegate` passes the device token to `AppModel`, which sends it in hex with `PUT /api/pair/<id>/push`, platform `apns` in Release builds (App Store and TestFlight) and `apns-dev` in Debug builds (Apple's sandbox); it is sent again only when the pairing, platform or token changes (`pushSent` in user defaults). Unpairing sends `DELETE …/push` before the pairing's own `DELETE`.
- **Content.** APNs brings fallback text ("New from your computer") with the sealed `PhonePush` (`b`, place `push`) and the pairing id (`p`). The Notification Service Extension `WorldletNotify` reads the pairing link from the Keychain, opens the box, shows its title and body under thread `worldlet` and puts where a tap goes in userInfo (`open`); a box it cannot open keeps the fallback, without buttons. Both targets read the link through the keychain access group `$(AppIdentifierPrefix)app.worldlet.ios` in their entitlements, the app's own group, where it always kept the link, so phones paired before notifications need nothing moved.
- **Buttons.** The extension also sets the notification's category from the sealed `act` (`PhonePush.category`), and `AppDelegate` registers the categories at launch (`PushAction.buttons`): Done and Later on an item, Reply (a `UNTextInputNotificationAction`) on Fox's answer, Allow once and Deny on what the person's own Agent asks (Deny alone when Allow once is not offered). Reply and Allow once are `.authenticationRequired`, Deny `.destructive`; none is `.foreground`, so iOS wakes the app in the background, `AppModel.answerNotification` opens the box again, and the same message as the app's own button (`PhonePush.message`) goes to the computer before the handler returns. A launch in the background does not start the long poll, so the computer still counts the app as closed. When the message cannot go, a local notification with the same buttons says "Not sent" ([Actions](../core/phone/README.md#actions); tests in `PushTests`).
- **Tap.** A tap opens the item's card, the Applet's page or Fox's dialogue expanded (an approval's notification opens the dialogue with its card); an item or Applet the Center does not have yet waits for the next snapshot, and one still missing then opens nothing. While the app is open a notification shows nothing, since the live Center and Fox already do.
- **Entitlements.** `aps-environment` (development in the project; the App Store export's profile makes it production) and `keychain-access-groups`. Automatic signing (`-allowProvisioningUpdates`) registers the extension's bundle and the push capability. The relay needs the team's APNs key to send.

## Build and test

- `swift test` in this folder runs WorldletKit's tests (Mac or Linux).
- On a Mac with Xcode: `brew install xcodegen`, then `xcodegen generate` here and open `Worldlet.xcodeproj` (generated, not committed).
- `-demo` shows fixed sample data without a computer or network; add `-page later` for the Later page, `-page applets` for the Applet world, `-applet <key>` (for example `gmail`) for an Applet's page or `-card <id>` (for example `t1`, or `e1` with an earlier turn) for an item's card and Fox's dialogue about it, `-widget wgt-demogetty1` for the demo widget open, or `-recording holding|locked` for the voice controls.
- **Try the demo** on the pairing screen (owner request 2026-10-04, so App Review and people without Worldlet on a computer can see the app) loads the same sample data with no computer and no network: Done, Later and Dismiss work on it, Fox answers every line with a note that this is the demo, and Settings offers Leave the demo, which goes back to pairing.
- `UITests/` holds the XCUITest UI tests: on `-demo` data they tap through pairing, Try the demo and leaving it, Now and an item's card, opening and closing a widget with Fox's dialogue about it, the Later page, typing to Fox in its input bar, and Settings from the computer button and from Fox. `npm run test:ios` (repository root, on a Mac with Xcode) runs WorldletKit's tests and these UI tests on an iPhone Simulator; the Mac RC runs it for every RC.
- Pull requests run nothing for the iPhone app (owner decision 2026-10-04: PR CI is Linux basic checks only, no macOS runner). WorldletKit's tests, the Simulator build and the UI tests run in the Mac RC as `npm run test:ios`.

## Distribution

On TestFlight (internal testing) since 2026-10-03: App Store Connect app "Worldlet", bundle `app.worldlet.ios`, SKU `worldlet-ios`, team N9KJV72CSL. Submitted to App Store review on 2026-10-04 (owner request) and live on the App Store since 2026-10-04; the App Store version is the build's full version, like TestFlight's.

**Uploads follow the desktop channels** (owner decisions 2026-10-03: no asking; 2026-10-06: "TestFlight internal 在02和 alpha一起发布, TestFlight external 在02和 beta一起发布"). On the Mac release host (02) each machine-daemon tick runs scripts/machine-testflight.mjs: when a passed Mac RC reaches Alpha, it archives that RC's commit in a temporary worktree and uploads it with `ExportOptions.plist`, using the Mac's signed-in Xcode, so internal testers get each Alpha. When a Mac release is published (Beta), that release's build goes to the public beta, uploaded first if it never was. The build number is the desktop Build (`CURRENT_PROJECT_VERSION`) and the App Store version (`MARKETING_VERSION`) the desktop version without the leading zero (`2026.1006.2979`), so one number names the same commit on every platform; each build goes through Beta App Review on its own. It waits while a release job runs. State is in `.local/testflight.json` (`internal` and `external` are the newest builds of each; log `.local/testflight.log`); a failure retries an hour later; `.local/testflight-off` turns it off. 02 needs `xcodegen` and the App Store Connect API key below: archive and upload sign in with it (`-authenticationKeyPath`, cloud signing; the key needs the Admin role for that), so no Apple ID has to be signed in to Xcode. Without the key, Xcode must be signed in to team N9KJV72CSL.

**The public beta** step is [scripts/testflight-distribute.mjs](../scripts/testflight-distribute.mjs): it waits for App Store Connect to process the build, sets its What to Test to the commit subject, adds it to the external TestFlight groups (those with a public link; or the `groups` named in the key file) and submits it for Beta App Review. It uses an App Store Connect API key (App Manager role) on 02: `.local/app-store-connect.json` with `{"issuerId","keyId"}` and the `.p8` at `~/.appstoreconnect/private_keys/AuthKey_<keyId>.p8` (or `keyPath`). Without the key, builds reach internal testers only and the board shows `no key`; a failed beta step shows `beta: failed` with the error in `.local/testflight.json` (`betaError`) and retries an hour later without uploading again. Export compliance is declared in `project.yml` (`ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption: false`).

By hand, from a Mac whose Xcode is signed in to the team (if `xcode-select` points at the Command Line Tools, prefix with `DEVELOPER_DIR=/Applications/Xcode.app/Contents/Developer`):

1. `xcodegen generate`.
2. `xcodebuild -project Worldlet.xcodeproj -scheme Worldlet -configuration Release -destination 'generic/platform=iOS' -archivePath build/Worldlet.xcarchive -allowProvisioningUpdates CURRENT_PROJECT_VERSION=$(git rev-list --count HEAD) archive` (App Store Connect rejects a build number it already has).
3. `xcodebuild -exportArchive -archivePath build/Worldlet.xcarchive -exportOptionsPlist ExportOptions.plist -exportPath build/export -allowProvisioningUpdates` uploads it; processing takes a few minutes, then testers in the TestFlight internal group get it.
